# From checked finite data to constructed arithmetic objects

This milestone adds 113 Lean theorems to the previous 153-theorem package.
The project now has 266 theorems across 15 modules. The original 15 theorems
remain byte-identical to the coordinated handoff; 251 are new in this
formalization branch. The build, full theorem axiom inventory, source hashes,
and separate module kernel replays are recorded in
[`verification/receipt.json`](verification/receipt.json).

## 1. Root existence is now an actual theorem

Previously, `BSDHenselWitness` proved residual divisibility and derivative
valuation checks for 147 numerical approximations. It did not apply Hensel's
lemma to construct roots.

The new chain is:

1. `BSDHenselSemantics` interprets the full signed integer lists as Mathlib
   polynomials, proves the derivative-list interpretation, and proves that
   the candidates are monic of degrees seven and 140 in every nontrivial
   commutative coefficient ring.
2. Exact residue computations transfer to actual polynomial divisibility.
   The derivative has the form `433^s v`; a certified quadratic norm makes
   the residue of v a unit modulo `433^4`.
3. `BSDHenselLift` constructs the nontrivial ring
   `AdicCompletion (Ideal.span {433^4}) (Zsqrtd 5)` and its completeness
   instance. A proved scaled Hensel theorem constructs roots of the full
   polynomials.
4. `BSDHenselDistinct` transports congruences through the completion's
   first quotient map. The existing `Nodup` canonical tables then prove
   injectivity of each root family and the exact canonical residue labels.

Writing C for that completed quadratic order and P71/P44375 for the full
candidate polynomials, the resulting unconditional statements provide

\[
r_{71}:\operatorname{Fin}(7)\longrightarrow C,
\qquad
r_{44375}:\operatorname{Fin}(140)\longrightarrow C,
\]

with each map injective and

\[
P_{71}(r_{71}(i))=0,\qquad
P_{44375}(r_{44375}(j))=0.
\]

Each root reduces to its specified original residue pair modulo `433^4`.
No approximate-root or derivative-unit hypothesis remains for these supplied
polynomials and labels.

The proof does not assert that C is an integral domain, identify it with a
packaged unramified quadratic extension of the 433-adic rationals, or prove
that these are exhaustive root lists in an arbitrary ring. Distinctness is
within each family, not a cross-family theorem. The candidates have not yet
been identified in Lean with analytic Hilbert class polynomials.

### The scaled Hensel argument

The reusable theorem works in an adically complete commutative ring R with
ideal I. It takes actual identities

\[
f(a)=d^2e,\qquad f'(a)=dv,
\]

where `e ∈ I` and the image of v in `R/I` is a unit. Taylor expansion gives

\[
f(a+dY)=d^2\bigl(e+vY+Y^2h(dY)\bigr).
\]

The normalized polynomial has a simple root modulo I. A Newton argument
constructs its root in R, then substitution gives a root of f congruent to
a modulo I. The proof does not divide by a possibly nonunit d.

For our data, I is generated by `433^4`, d is `433^s`, and the checked bound
`2s+4 ≤ 24` supplies exactly the required residual divisibility. The simple
Newton proof is adapted, with attribution, from Mathlib's Henselian module.
No new arithmetic axiom is introduced.

## 2. Ordinary parameters come from the actual curves

`BSDOrdinary` uses these models:

\[
E_{433}:y^2+xy=x^3+1,
\qquad E_{389}:y^2+y=x^3+x^2-2x.
\]

It proves their integer discriminants are -433 and 389, verifies ellipticity
of their reductions modulo five, and counts Mathlib's actual affine point
types with the point at infinity included:

\[
\#E_{433}(\mathbb F_5)=10,\qquad
\#E_{389}(\mathbb F_5)=9.
\]

The traces defined by `5+1-#E(F5)` are therefore -4 and -3. Hensel's lemma
constructs roots in the actual `PadicInt 5` ring and proves uniqueness among
unit roots:

\[
\alpha_{433}^2+4\alpha_{433}+5=0,
\qquad
\alpha_{389}^2+3\alpha_{389}+5=0.
\]

The parameters and their unit properties are conclusions. The assembled
E433 certificate uses the resulting unit `alpha433^(-2)` and proves its
inverse-square identity. Its identification with the chosen arithmetic
theta normalization still belongs to the finite-family identification.

## 3. The infinite series is constructed from finite representatives

Let `G n` be a polynomial over the 5-adic integers representing the cyclic
quotient of order `5^n`, and put

\[
\omega_n=(1+T)^{5^n}-1.
\]

The distribution hypothesis is the finite polynomial divisibility

\[
\omega_n\mid G_{n+1}-G_n\quad\text{for every }n.
\]

The implementation derives compatibility between all pairs of levels.
Prime-power binomial divisibility makes each coefficient sequence Cauchy;
Mathlib's adic completeness constructs each limiting 5-adic coefficient.
`thetaLimit` assembles those coefficients into a genuine power series.

Its uniqueness and precision are proved. At the first useful level, the
construction supplies integral power series H and J satisfying

\[
F-G_1=\bigl((1+T)^5-1\bigr)H+5J.
\]

Neither F nor those error witnesses are inputs to the final theorem.

### Exact zeros from finite identities at all levels

The finite symmetry hypothesis allows an origin multiplier U_n with
`U_n(0)=1` and requires

\[
\omega_n\mid
G_n(T)-U_n(T)G_n\bigl((1+T)^{5^n-1}-1\bigr).
\]

Compatibility preserves augmentation exactly. If `G_0(0)=0`, the limit has
constant coefficient zero. At degree one, the finite inverse has coefficient
`5^n-1`, and symmetry implies

\[
5^n\mid 2[T]G_n.
\]

Passing to the proved coefficient limit and using 5-adic separation gives
`[T]F=0` exactly. No infinite functional equation is assumed, and no finite
precision zero is promoted to an exact zero.

These assumptions describe finite objects at every level. They still
require uniform arithmetic proofs for all levels. Checking the conductor-25
table alone does not prove the distribution or symmetry hypotheses.

### The assembled certificate

`BSDArithmeticCertificate.e433_certificate_of_finite_tower` takes:

- A polynomial family G and its successive distribution relations.
- The finite symmetries above and zero level-zero augmentation.
- The five congruences through degree four modulo five identifying G1 with
  the regularized first-level element from the canonical 140-value table,
  using the constructed ordinary scale `alpha433^(-2)`.

It constructs F and proves

\[
\operatorname{ord}_T F=2,
\qquad F=T^2V,
\qquad V(0)\in\mathbb Z_5^\times.
\]

This is a stronger interface than the preceding arbitrary-series theorem:
ordinary-unit existence, the infinite series, both exact lower-order zeros,
and the integral approximation are now derived. Constructing and identifying
the actual arithmetic family G remains open.

## 4. A statement restriction was detected and corrected

An exact first-level equality to the regularized polynomial, combined with
compatibility and zero augmentation, forces its lower-level correction c to
be zero. `trace_scale_zero_of_exact_first_level` now proves this consequence.

The intended certificate only needs first-level coefficient congruences
modulo five. The new interface uses precisely those congruences. Its inputs
are jointly consistent for arbitrary c: Lean proves that the constant tower

\[
G_n=u(T^2+T^3)-cT^4
\]

has the required symmetry with multiplier `(1+T)^4`, zero augmentation and
compatibility. Its difference from the regularized expression is

\[
5c(1+2T+2T^2+T^3).
\]

This is a formal consistency witness for the interface. It is not a claim
that the arithmetic CM tower is constant.

## 5. What remains before an arithmetic theta theorem

The finite computations now connect to actual roots and actual 5-adic
objects. The remaining arithmetic identification is substantial:

- Identify the full candidate polynomials and root labels with CM invariants.
- Identify the matrix and eigenfunction with the curve's Brandt and
  Jacquet-Langlands objects.
- Construct the actual ring-class distributions with coherent generators
  and origins, and prove their distribution and finite symmetry identities
  uniformly over all levels.
- Prove the exact level-zero augmentation and the first-level match in the
  assembled theorem.

The series construction here is not yet a formal ring equivalence between
the entire Iwasawa inverse limit and `Z5[[T]]`, nor an all-level exact
quotient-reduction theorem. It proves the convergence, uniqueness, low-degree
precision and integral approximation needed for this certificate.

The Selmer/Kummer arithmetic bridges and the passage to the desired rank and
Sha statement are also still separate. Universal BSD and its complex
L-function order are not proved by this milestone.
