# From a projective regulator direction to nonzero localization

Date: 2026-11-27. This is an independent logic audit of the comparison
program. It adds a sufficient nonvanishing theorem and exact
countermodels; it does not assume that the global-class or full local
comparison has already been proved.

## Results

Three possible logical obstacles can be separated sharply.

1. Projective continuation alone does not identify vanishing orders.
   For the same CH local class, however, the independent order-two theta
   identity forces the full regulator to have exact order two. No order
   equality needs to be inferred from projective continuation.
2. A nonzero complementary quadratic coefficient forces nonzero finite
   localization without an elementary-divisor or simple-determinant
   theorem for the relative Lubin--Tate regulator. That stronger local
   theorem is needed for a universal calibrated formula, not for this
   one-way implication.
3. In the specified alternating Weil/cup identification, the line
   generated by zeta is both the annihilating test-vector line and the
   base output-image line. There is no missing inverse-transpose in this
   identification. Arbitrary semilinear averaging, in contrast, does not
   preserve a projective line merely because one tensor factor has rank
   one.

The remaining application still needs the actual arithmetic family and
the full normalized local comparison. These lemmas do not manufacture
either comparison from a scalar reciprocity law.

## 1. The local algebra needs no determinant hypothesis

Let R=L[[t]], or the ring of analytic germs at zero over a p-adic field
L. Let M be an R-module with a specialization map

    sp: M -> H,             ker(sp)=tM.

Let H_f be a finite local subspace of H, and suppose its logarithm
ell:H_f->L is injective. For the local elliptic representation over
Q_p in this project, H_f is a line and the formal/Bloch--Kato logarithm
is its nonzero coordinate. Let D be a finite-dimensional L-vector
space and let

    P: M -> R tensor_L D

be R-linear and regular at zero. Assume its base specialization P0
factors through sp and kills H_f. Let lambda:D->L be a constant linear
functional such that

    lambda P0 = 0 on H.

Suppose y=t x in M and x0=sp(x) belongs to H_f. Then:

**Quadratic nonvanishing criterion.**

    [t^2] lambda P(y) != 0   implies   ell(x0) != 0.       (1)

### Proof by cancellation

Since lambda P vanishes at the base on every input, its image is
contained in tR. Hence

    G=lambda P/t: M -> R

is a regular R-linear map. Because G(tM) is contained in tR, its base
value factors through M/tM and therefore through the image of sp.
R-linearity now gives

    [t^2] lambda P(y) = [t] lambda P(x) = G0(x0).         (2)

If the left side is nonzero, x0 is nonzero. Since x0 is finite and ell
is injective there, ell(x0) is nonzero. This proves (1).

Equivalently, if x0=0 then x=t z by the specialization exact sequence;
thus y=t^2 z and lambda P(y) belongs to t^3 R. This contrapositive
requires no freeness or rank assumption on M.

### What is not required

The proof does not require det(P) to have a simple zero, does not need
the value of a calibration constant c_P, and does not assert that G0 is
nonzero on H_f before evaluating it on the actual x0. In particular it
does not invoke a cyclotomic elementary-divisor theorem as a theorem
about the relative Lubin--Tate map.

If M and the output are free of rank two and P0 has rank one, a successful
test in (1) actually implies the simple-determinant property: in a basis
adapted to ker(P0)=H_f and im(P0), the first determinant coefficient is a
nonzero scalar times G0 on the finite basis vector. Thus the successful
test supplies a witness to that property; it need not be assumed first.

For an exact equality ell(x0)=c_P^(-1)[t^2]lambda P(y), valid for every
finite x0 with a specified nonzero c_P, the stronger comparison in
`../finite_log_reciprocity/FINITE_COORDINATE.md` remains relevant. The
present criterion proves only the nonvanishing implication needed here.

### Why a zero test remains inconclusive

Take M=R^2, H_f=L e1, lambda(u,v)=u and

    P_m=diag(t^m,1),        y=(a t,t^2),       m>=1.

The singular scalar coordinate is always t^2, and ell(sp(y/t))=a.
For m=1, the complementary quadratic coefficient equals a. For m=2,
that coefficient is zero even when a=1. Thus the direction test is
sufficient when nonzero; a zero result would require more information.
These are algebraic countermodels, not asserted elliptic realizations.

## 2. The theta identity fixes the actual vector order

In the setting above, P0(x0)=0 gives

    P(y)=t P(x) in t^2(R tensor D).

Suppose one fixed scalar functional mu on D satisfies the actual
reciprocity identity

    mu(P(y))=u(t) Theta(t),       u(0)!=0,
    ord_t Theta=2.

Then P(y) has exact order two: it has order at least two by the local
condition and at most two because the displayed scalar coordinate does.
Its leading vector v2=[t^2]P(y) is therefore nonzero.

For the named CH family, the scalar input is Corollary 3.7 with the
chosen test vectors and the supplied theta-order certificate. The zero
local base and finite local condition of x0 are the previously stated
inputs of `../derived_global/DERIVED_CLASSES.md` Section 6. If a different
global family is adopted, these facts must be established for that family
before invoking this lemma.

This resolves the order issue without strengthening the projective
continuation lemma. For comparison, W^2 v and W^3 v have the same
projective line and different orders. The independent theta identity
rules out that ambiguity for the actual regulator in this application.

## 3. The base output line and its annihilator

Use the self-dual elliptic identification and its nondegenerate alternating
de Rham pairing < , >. Set

    A_phi=(1-p^(-1) phi^(-1))(1-phi)^(-1).

The source is Castella--Hsieh, Definition 3.3, equations (3.7)--(3.9),
and Theorem 3.4 at j=0,n=-1, printed pages 15--16:

https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/Kato-print.pdf

Using both independent Frobenius test vectors and linearity, the source
formula is the vector identity

    P0(z)=A_phi^dagger exp*(z0).                          (3)

Here any fixed Tate trivialization is used consistently on both sides.
The dual exponential has image in the one-dimensional finite Hodge
line L omega_tilde and kills H_f. Since a line in a symplectic plane is
its own annihilator, if

    zeta=A_phi^(-1) omega_tilde,

then <P0(z),zeta>=<exp*(z0),omega_tilde>=0 for all z.
Therefore lambda(v)=<v,zeta> is exactly a functional allowed in (1).

Moreover every invertible endomorphism A of a symplectic plane satisfies

    A^dagger=det(A) A^(-1).

Thus the image line in (3) is L zeta, whenever the base image is nonzero.
One does not have to guess whether the annihilating test vector and
the output vector transform by an inverse or an adjoint: in dimension
two they give the same line, with the displayed determinant scalar.

### Exact pilot calculation

Use phi=F/5 and F^2+3F+5I=0. Then

    A_phi=(59I+13F)/45,
    A_phi^(-1)=(20I-13F)/45,
    det(A_phi)=1,
    A_phi^dagger=A_phi^(-1).

Consequently

    zeta=(20I-13F)omega_tilde/45.

These equalities are checked exactly in Q[F]/(F^2+3F+5). In the formal
cyclic basis (omega,F omega), F has matrix [[0,-5],[1,-3]]. The actual
saved Frobenius matrix has a nonzero lower first-column entry, so that
cyclic basis is valid for the pilot over Q5. This statement does not
assert it is an integral basis.

## 4. The combined projective criterion

Let A(W) be the computed analytic vector, with exact order two and
nonzero quadratic coefficient A2. Let K(W) be an analytic invertible
comparison matrix. Suppose the actual same-class comparison proves the
germ identity

    P(y(t(W))) wedge K(W)A(W)=0,                         (4)

where t(W)=a W+O(W^2), a!=0. The superflat argument in
`../fixed_weight_syntomic/PROJECTIVE_INTERPOLATION.md` is a sufficient
way to prove (4) from full classical specializations. It need not control
the varying pointwise proportionality scalars.

By Section 2, P(y) has exact order two independently of (4). Taking the
leading coefficient of the wedge gives

    a^2 v2 is a nonzero scalar multiple of K(0) A2.

Using the base annihilator lambda(v)=<v,zeta>, the criterion (1) yields

    <K(0) A2,zeta> != 0  implies  ell(sp(y/t)) != 0.     (5)

Equivalently, in any fixed two-dimensional coordinate basis,

    det(A2, K(0)^(-1) zeta) != 0

is sufficient. Only the nonzero determinant is used; its absolute
period normalization and the value of a^2 are unnecessary for
nonvanishing. This is why a proved projective comparison is enough.

For the conditional K0 of `../pr_syntomic_comparison/README.md`, the
saved cubic calculation shows separation from K0^(-1)zeta. That fact
is still conditional on identifying (4) for the actual family. The
algebra here removes an additional simple-determinant premise; it
does not prove (4) from numerical separation.

## 5. Unit rescaling of the global family

Let c be L-linear and semilinear for t->-t, and put

    x=(kappa-c(kappa))/t.

Assume kappa0 is c-invariant and locally strict. If kappa'=u(t)kappa
for a regular unit u=u0+u1 t+..., then

    x'_0=u0 x0+2u1 kappa0.                              (6)

Indeed c(u kappa)=u(-t)c(kappa); the first coefficient of u(t)-u(-t)
is 2u1. Since kappa0 has zero local class, (6) implies

    log(loc x'_0)=u0 log(loc x0).

Thus nonzero localization is unchanged by a unit scalar comparison,
even when u is not an even function. The derived classes themselves
can differ by a multiple of the strict base class. This supports a
global comparison up to a unit; it does not prove that two proposed
families differ by such a unit.

## 6. What rank-one periods do and do not imply

For an already identified tensor decomposition D tensor L_CM with
L_CM a one-dimensional de Rham line, changing the basis of L_CM
multiplies the whole D-vector by one nonzero scalar. It cannot change
its projective direction.

This statement must not be applied to an arbitrary semilinear average.
For example, let u^2=2 over Q5, sigma(u)=-u and v=(1,u). The raw trace
is v+sigma(v)=(2,0), which is not proportional to v. Adding a trivial
rank-one tensor factor changes nothing about this counterexample.

A weighted conjugate sum preserves the original line if each conjugate
already lies on that same line, and the final scalar sum is nonzero.
Such a line-descent statement is an additional fact, not a consequence
of the phrase "rank-one CM factor".

Nor may Galois transport be replaced by geometric crystalline F. For
the pilot companion matrix F=[[0,-5],[1,-3]], the vector v=(1,1) is
sent by I+F to (-4,-1), and their determinant is 3. Thus a polynomial
in F can change the line.

The correct economical approach for CH is to use Theorem 3.4 itself:
it is already the interpolation theorem for the fully normalized map
(3.9), including sigma0 and h_e. Taking both independent test vectors
reconstructs its full vector. Only after that theorem and the actual
CM-component identification are applied is a rank-one period change
legitimately a scalar. This audit does not replace (3.9) by a trace.

## 7. The actual corrected ratio cancels to a rational residue

The independently implemented cubic multiplication in `check_logic.py`
applies the conditional PR matrix to the actual saved ratio. Modulo125,
the U roots are alpha=32 and beta=90. The F eigenvalue on f_alpha is
beta, and on f_beta it is alpha, so the correction to b/a is

    k(alpha)/k(beta),
    k(X)=(5 tau+11)+2(tau+1)X,
    tau=1-xi-xi^2.

The numerator and denominator in the cubic basis are respectively

    k(alpha)=(19,56,56),       k(beta)=(1,65,65).

An explicit denominator inverse is (101,110,10). Exact multiplication
therefore gives

    k(alpha)/k(beta)=(79,1,91) mod125,
    (79,1,91)*(109,75,106)=(21,0,0) mod125.             (7)

Thus the conditionally corrected scaled stabilization ratio is the
rational residue 21 modulo125, at every available digit. This is an
observed cancellation at finite precision; it is not a proof of exact
coefficient-field descent.

Independently, the saved crystalline zeta vector converts to

    zeta=(19330,67476) in the stabilization basis mod5^7.

Its first coordinate has valuation one and its second has valuation
zero. Hence

    5 r_zeta=67476/(19330/5)=86 mod125.

The resulting corrected diagnostic is especially simple:

    5 r_corrected - 5 r_zeta = 21-86 = 60 mod125.       (8)

It has valuation one. Multiplying the previously recorded cubic
difference (115,0,35) by (79,1,91) produces exactly (60,0,0), supplying
another consistency check of the direction and the F/U slope labels.
Equations (7)--(8) become the arithmetic determinant test only after

## 8. Generic rank one can replace literal equality of global classes

Let Lambda be the one-variable coefficient Iwasawa domain, S a
finitely generated module with generic rank one, and C a domain of
analytic germs receiving an injective map from Lambda. Suppose

    P: S -> C tensor D

is Lambda-linear. If s1 and s2 are non-torsion elements of S, their
images are proportional over Frac(Lambda), after ignoring torsion.
Clearing denominators and annihilating the remaining torsion gives
nonzero a,b in Lambda with

    a s1=b s2.

Since the target is torsion-free, a P(s1)=b P(s2). If either regulator
image is nonzero, both are nonzero and define the same meromorphic
projective line. A scalar zero or pole at augmentation does not change
that line. The identity also holds at all sufficiently close nonzero
arithmetic points where the scalars are defined.

Therefore a published generic-rank-one result for the *same* rel,str
module could replace a literal comparison of the two cycle
constructions. This is an additional theorem about their common module,
not an inference from agreement of ordinary scalar values.

One may retain the CH class for reflection and its theta-order argument,
and use the geometric family's full specializations only to identify
the common leading projective line. The scalar ratio need not be a
unit, because Section2 independently determines the CH regulator's
order. In this route the unit-rescaling lemma of Section5 is useful but
not necessary.

The proof of Castella--Do Theorem5.6.1 explicitly gives rank one for
Sel_rel,str(K,T_f,chi), under its stated hypotheses and big-image input:

https://kimtuando.github.io/website/Schoen_CM.pdf

Its application must check the coefficient representation, finite
character, anticyclotomic parameter, and local conditions for both
classes. In particular the second local condition is **zero full
localization**, not merely membership in an ordinary line. The present
lemma does not certify those memberships. The companion global-class
workstream handles that source comparison.

## 9. Away-from-p local conditions after localization at augmentation

There is a useful rational simplification for checking that common
module. Let A be the localization of Lambda[1/p] at the augmentation
prime, a local DVR with residue field L. For a finite place v not
dividing p, suppose the local cohomology of the universal deformation is
represented by a perfect A-complex C_v, with the usual derived
specialization

    C_v tensor_A^L L = R Gamma(K_v,V_pE tensor L).

The special fiber on the right is acyclic. Indeed E(K_v)[p^infinity]
is finite, giving H0=0; elliptic Weil self-duality and local Tate
duality give H2=0; and the local Euler characteristic at residue
characteristic different from p is zero, giving H1=0. This includes
multiplicative reduction. At a split multiplicative prime the Tate
parameter has a fixed nonzero valuation, so it cannot supply compatible
rational p-power torsion of all orders.

For the local duality and Euler characteristic inputs, see Milne,
*Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, second edition, I.2, especially
Corollary2.3 and Theorem2.8 (and its prime-to-residue-characteristic
Lemma2.9), applied to E[p^n] and passed to the Tate module:

https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/BSDseminar/refs/MilneADT.pdf

Derived Nakayama therefore gives C_v=0 over A. For a fixed finite
ramification set, localization is flat and commutes with the kernel
defining the Selmer module. Thus the away-from-p conditions are
automatic in this augmentation germ. Integral local torsion or
Tamagawa factors are not being declared zero before inverting p.

In this perfect local situation the Nakayama step is elementary:
represent C_v by a bounded complex of finite free modules and cancel
every unit entry in its differentials by splitting off a contractible
two-term summand. The remaining minimal complex has all differential
entries in the maximal ideal. Its special fiber has zero differentials;
acyclicity of that fiber forces every remaining free module to have
rank zero. Hence the original complex is contractible.

This argument explicitly retains perfectness and derived local
base-change, which hold for the usual finite-rank local Galois complex
used in this deformation. It is a germ statement; it does not assert
vanishing of every integral local Iwasawa module over the whole
weight space.

## 10. Scope and reproduction

The mathematical statements above are proofs with explicit hypotheses.
The executable `check_logic.py` checks the exact pilot adjoint algebra,
the sharp algebraic countermodels, and the rescaling example. It is not
a formal proof of CH, the global family comparison, or a new arithmetic
nonzero-localization result.

    python research/bsd-localization/comparison_logic_audit/check_logic.py --check

Primary source facts used here are limited to the full CH interpolation
formula, its vector-map definition, and the ordinary theta reciprocity
identity. The specialization exactness, reflected-class construction,
and analytic projective lemma are the project results explicitly cited
above. No simple-determinant theorem for a different local tower is
imported.
