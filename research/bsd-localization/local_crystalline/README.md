# Local Frobenius and a nonzero cyclotomic height on the strict line

These are new exact computations for the pilot
`E: y^2+y=x^3+x^2-2x`, at `p=5`. They supply the local splitting and
an independent height benchmark for future regulator calculations.
Neither computation identifies the localization of a global diagonal class.

## 1. Crystalline Frobenius

Put `Y=2y+1`, so `Y^2=f(x)=4x^3+4x^2-8x+1`. In the ordered basis

\[
\omega=dx/Y=dx/(2y+1),\qquad \eta=x\omega,
\]

the column-convention Frobenius matrix is

\[
F\equiv\begin{pmatrix}285070&163221\\276685&105552\end{pmatrix}
\pmod{5^8},\qquad 5^8=390625.
\]

The ordinary root of `X^2+3X+5` is `alpha=249782 mod 5^8`, and its
eigenline is generated by

\[
\eta+306458\,\omega\pmod{5^8}.
\]

Trace `-3`, determinant `5`, the finite-field point count `9`, and the
eigenvector equation are checked independently in the script. This is the
unit-root line in `H^1_dR(E)`. A Tate twist changes Frobenius slopes: it
must not be confused with an unadjusted slope statement for `V_pE`.

### The complementary interpolation pairing is now explicit

On `V=H^1_et(E)(1)`, write `phi=F/5` and suppress the Tate-twist basis
symbol. In the base-character case of
[Castella--Hsieh Theorem 3.4](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/Kato-print.pdf),
the scalar pairing with a vector `v` applies the operator

\[
A_\varphi=(1-5^{-1}\varphi^{-1})(1-\varphi)^{-1}
\]

to that vector. Since the finite Hodge line is isotropic for the de Rham
pairing, taking `A_phi*zeta=omega` makes this scalar regulator vanish at
the base character for every local cohomology class. Hence its division
by the augmentation parameter is the concrete candidate for a finite
coordinate regulator. The vector is

\[
\zeta=(1-F/5)(1-F^{-1})^{-1}\omega
       =\frac{20I-13F}{45}\omega
       \equiv13133\,\omega+32916\,\eta\pmod{5^7}.
\]

The simplification uses `F^2+3F+5I=0`. Division by 5 loses one absolute
digit, which is explicitly recorded in the output. This vector generally
mixes the two eigenlines; choosing the other eigenvector alone would not
implement the required interpolation annihilator.

This identifies the pairing to investigate. It does **not** prove that
its divided regulator restricts to a specified nonzero multiple of the
finite logarithm in the relative local tower, and it does not evaluate
that pairing on the global diagonal class. Those exact comparison
requirements are in
[`FINITE_COORDINATE.md`](../finite_log_reciprocity/FINITE_COORDINATE.md).

### Algorithm and rigorous precision

The script uses exact rational polynomial arithmetic throughout the
cohomological reduction, then reduces each resulting coefficient modulo
`5^8`. There is no floating-point rounding. It implements the Frobenius
lift used in [Kedlaya's paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105031):

\[
F(x^i dx/Y)=5x^{5i+4}\sum_{k\geq0}
\binom{-1/2}{k}\bigl(f(x^5)-f(x)^5\bigr)^k
\frac{dx}{Y^{5(2k+1)}}.
\]

For `m=5k+2`, write a numerator as `P=A f+B f'`, with `deg B<3`.
The vertical reduction, modulo exact differentials, is

\[
P\frac{dx}{Y^{2m+1}}\sim
\left(A+\frac{2B'}{2m-1}\right)\frac{dx}{Y^{2m-1}}.
\]

Horizontal reduction uses
`2d(x^d Y)=(2d x^(d-1) f+x^d f')dx/Y`.
The inverse of `f'` modulo `f` is computed by the Euclidean algorithm,
and its defining identity is checked exactly.

Here is a tail argument, needed in addition to finite congruence checks.
The polynomial `f(x^5)-f(x)^5` is divisible by 5; the binomial coefficient
is 5-integral. The kth numerator therefore has valuation at least `k+1`
and degree at most `15k+9`. Since the leading coefficient 4 of `f` is a
5-adic unit, repeated polynomial division expands this numerator in
powers of `f` with coefficients of degree at most two, without losing
precision. Terms with nonpositive powers of `Y` have pole index at most
`m=5k+2`. Kedlaya's Lemma 2 bounds the loss in reducing such a term by
`floor(log_5(2m+1))`. The change `Y/2` makes the cubic monic, so its
normalization does not affect this bound.

The only possible positive-power term is a constant multiple of `Y dx`.
It has the explicit reduction

\[
Y\,dx\sim\left(\frac{17}{15}-\frac{56}{15}x\right)dx/Y,
\]

which loses at most one 5-adic digit. The previous logarithmic bound is
at least one, so every kth term has reduced valuation at least

\[
k+1-\lfloor\log_5(10k+5)\rfloor.
\]

This lower bound is nondecreasing and tends to infinity. For `k>=9` it
is at least 8. Thus terms `k=0,...,8` determine the matrix modulo `5^8`.
This proof uses Lemma 2 and the displayed explicit positive-power
reduction; it does not assume a generic denominator bound for positive
powers. The computation is a Python certificate interpreted using this
classical argument, not a Lean kernel proof.

### Independent published calibration

Run the same implementation on `37a1`, using `f=(1,-4,0,4)`. At precision
`5^6=15625`, it gives

\[
F_{37}=\begin{pmatrix}1465&5187\\8945&14158\end{pmatrix},
\qquad s_{37}=14769.
\]

Consequently `E2=-12*s37=10272 mod15625`. This equals the independently
published Sage expansion
`2+4*5+2*5^3+5^4+3*5^5 mod5^6`. See the
[official E2 documentation](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/arithmetic_curves/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/ell_rational_field.html#sage.schemes.elliptic_curves.ell_rational_field.EllipticCurve_rational_field.padic_E2).
For our model, shifting to short x-coordinate `X=x+1/3` gives
`E2=4-12*s=228758 mod5^8`. The sigma differential-equation constant
`(a1^2+4a2-E2)/12` is therefore `s`.

## 2. Exact leading cyclotomic height

Let `P=(0,0)` and `Q=(1,0)`. In Sage's height normalization, the result is

\[
H_{\rm cyc}(P,Q)\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}0&15\\15&20\end{pmatrix}\pmod{25}.
\]

This means the entire pairing matrix has entries in `5 Z_5`, with

\[
H_{\rm cyc}/5\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&3\\3&4\end{pmatrix}\pmod5,
\qquad \det(H_{\rm cyc})/25\equiv1\pmod5.
\]

The normalized strict vector is

\[
R=(\log_5 Q/5)P-(\log_5 P/5)Q.
\]

It lies in `E(Q) tensor Q_5`, not necessarily in `E(Q)`. Our exact
point-logarithm computation gives its coordinates `(3,-1) mod5`.
Consequently

\[
\boxed{h_{\rm cyc}(R,R)/5\equiv1\pmod5.}
\]

In particular its cyclotomic self-height is nonzero, with valuation one.
This is an actual arithmetic computation, independent of a proposed
global reflected class. The comparison to an anticyclotomic Bockstein
would require a separate theorem; it is not inferred here.

### Why only exact rational arithmetic is needed

The minimal model has discriminant 389 and bad fiber type `I1`, so the
Tamagawa number is one. Also `#E(F5)=9`. For `B` among the points used,
write `x([9]B)=a/d^2`, `y([9]B)=b/d^3`, with `d>0`, and `t=-x/y`.
The script checks these equalities, coprimality, `v5(t)>=1`, and the
nonsingular reduction condition at 389 directly. The sigma height formula
in the convention used here is

\[
h_5(B)=-\frac2{81}\log_5\frac{\sigma_5(t([9]B))}{d([9]B)}.
\]

[Harvey, section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3404) gives the positive
sign convention. We explicitly use its negative, as in the current
[Sage implementation](https://github.com/sagemath/sage/blob/develop/src/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/padics.py)
(`answer = -total.log() * 2 / n**2`). Thus the sign is specified, not
inferred from an ambiguous name for a height.

Mazur--Tate sigma is in `t+t^2 Z_5[[t]]`, and its coefficient of `t^2`
is `a1/2=0`; see Harvey's introduction and section 4. Therefore
`sigma(t)/t` is in `1+t^2 Z_5[[t]]`, whose logarithm vanishes modulo 25
for these parameters. For the unit `u=t/d`,

\[
\log_5(u)\equiv(u^4-1)/4\pmod{25}.
\]

The entire leading height calculation reduces to exact rational point
multiplication and this modular formula:

| B | d([9]B) | t/d mod25 | log(t/d) mod25 | h5(B) mod25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P | 496035 | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| Q | 124991065 | 9 | 15 | 20 |
| A=Q-P=(-1,1) | 917918950015 | 6 | 5 | 15 |
| P+Q | 12278804002050455 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Polarization gives `h(P,Q)=15 mod25`. The
[published Sage rank-two example](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/arithmetic_curves/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/ell_rational_field.html#sage.schemes.elliptic_curves.ell_rational_field.EllipticCurve_rational_field.padic_height_pairing_matrix)
uses basis `(A,Q)`, where its matrix is `[[15,5],[5,20]] mod25`.
Changing basis by `P=Q-A` reproduces our matrix. The saved result comes
from the independent exact point calculation, with this publication used
as a normalization and arithmetic cross-check.

## Reproduction

From the package root:

```sh
python local_crystalline/frobenius.py --check local_crystalline/result.json
python local_crystalline/height_leading.py --check local_crystalline/height-result.json
```

All arithmetic uses the Python standard library. The height script reuses
the previously checked group law in `logic/verify_logic.py`. The complete
runner also checks the Frobenius calibration against the published 37a1
E2 value. No external arithmetic software or conjectural BSD identity is
used to manufacture these residues.
