# A seven-value certificate for the auxiliary class-character L-values

Date: 2026-11-27. This is an exact cyclotomic reduction and a proposed arithmetic interface. It does not supply the seven Gross-point values, an optimal embedding, or a new L-value computation.

## The useful reduction

Let K = Q(sqrt(-71)), with class group generated by c and of order seven. Let phi be the correctly normalized rational quaternionic eigenfunction associated to E389.a1. After clearing a common denominator, let

\[
v_i=\phi(x_i)\in\mathbf Z,\qquad 0\le i\le6,
\]

where x_i is the Gross point obtained from a fixed optimal CM embedding by the action of c^i. Fix a primitive seventh root of unity zeta and define the nontrivial class-character periods

\[
P_j=\sum_{i=0}^6v_i\zeta^{ji},\qquad j=1,\ldots,6.
\]

**Proposition.** The following conditions are equivalent:

1. The seven integers v_i are not all equal.
2. P_j is nonzero for one j in {1,...,6}.
3. P_j is nonzero for every j in {1,...,6}.

Thus **one unequal pair of verified integral Gross-point values certifies all six nontrivial periods are nonzero**. No complex approximation or p-adic approximation is required for this step.

## Proof

The polynomial

\[
\Phi_7(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^6
\]

is the minimal polynomial over Q of zeta. For completeness, Phi_7(X+1) is Eisenstein at seven: its leading coefficient is one, all lower coefficients are divisible by seven, and its constant coefficient is seven, not divisible by 49.

For V(X) = sum v_i X^i, the degree is at most six. Consequently V(zeta) = 0 if and only if V is a rational multiple of Phi_7, which holds if and only if all v_i are equal. Every zeta^j with 1 <= j <= 6 is also primitive, or equivalently is a Galois conjugate of zeta, proving the other assertions.

An explicit certificate uses the remainder

\[
V(X)\bmod\Phi_7(X)=\sum_{i=0}^5(v_i-v_6)X^i.
\]

For P_j, first permute coefficients by i maps to ji modulo seven, then subtract the coefficient of X^6 from each of the first six coefficients.

### Why the argument remains valid inside Q_29

Since 29 is one modulo seven, every seventh root of unity lies in Q_29. Although Phi_7 splits over Q_29, it is still irreducible over **Q**. A polynomial with rational, in particular integer, coefficients that vanishes at one primitive seventh root in Q_29 must be divisible by Phi_7 over Q. The chosen embedding of Q(zeta) into Q_29 is injective.

The rational-coefficient condition is essential. Arbitrary Q_29-valued weights do not satisfy this criterion. For example, coefficients (-zeta,1,0,0,0,0,0) give a zero period at zeta but are not constant.

### An optional smaller witness

If an independent exact argument supplies

\[
\sum_{i=0}^6v_i=0,
\]

then one verified nonzero entry already suffices. Indeed, the only constant vector with zero sum over characteristic zero is the zero vector. In the proposed arithmetic setting, the trivial-character period should vanish because L(E/K,1) = L(E,1)L(E^K,1) = 0 and the relevant toric period formula identifies its square with that value. This is an additional identification/vanishing argument, not a property of arbitrary integer vectors.

## What this would establish arithmetically

For an unramified class character psi of order seven, complex conjugation inverts the ideal class group. Thus

\[
\chi=\psi/\psi^\tau=\psi^2
\]

is also nontrivial of order seven. Choosing psi(c) = zeta^j makes chi(c) = zeta^(2j), so all choices are covered by the six periods above.

Once the applicable Gross/Waldspurger formula and its nonzero local constants have been identified, a nonzero P_(2j) proves

\[
L(E/K,\chi,1)\ne0.
\]

This can discharge **one factor** of the auxiliary hypothesis in Castella–Hsieh:

\[
L(E^K,1)\,L(E/K,\chi,1)\ne0.
\]

It does not establish L(E^K,1) nonzero. That factor needs a separate exact special-value calculation or theorem; the neighboring [eligibility package](../eligibility/arithmetic/README.md) now supplies a positive rational interval for this twist. The vanishing of the trivial period gives no information about the twist factor, because L(E,1) is already zero.

Since the prime above 29 is nonprincipal and Cl(K) has prime order seven, every nontrivial class character is nontrivial on that prime. Thus the regularity condition chi(pbar) != 1 follows once the nonprincipality and class-group facts have been certified. The class-character values lie in mu_7 inside Q_29. The induced weight-one theta series is cuspidal because psi is not equal to psi^tau.

The prime ideal (29,omega-13) corresponds to (29,25,6), which reduces to (3,1,6), hence has class a^4. Its conjugate (29,omega-17) has class a^3. If psi(a) = zeta, then chi(a) = zeta^2, chi(p_13) = zeta, and chi(p_17) = zeta^6. One may choose the 29-adic embedding in which zeta reduces to 23, which has order seven in F_29.

These class coordinates fix the ordering of the desired seven values. They do not themselves provide the Gross-point evaluation map.

### The exact Gross formula and a convention-safe interface

Gross, *Heights and the Special Values of L-series*, Theorem 11.5 (printed page 170), gives in the prime-level inert setting

\[
L(f,A,1)=\frac{(f,f)}{u^2\sqrt d}
 \langle e_{f,A},e_{f,A}\rangle.
\]

Here e_A = sum_B A(B)^(-1) e_B is the weighted sum of the h_K Gross points, and e_(f,A) is its orthogonal projection to the f-eigenspace. The prefactor is positive. The literature audit checked this formula and its definitions on printed pages 167–170 of the primary scan; see [its exact-source ledger](../literature/THEOREM_LEDGER.md).

A safe implementation interface avoids any unproved identification of raw Brandt coordinates with function values. Choose a nonzero rational vector g spanning the one-dimensional f-eigenspace in Gross's **weighted** Brandt space and set

\[
v_i=\langle e_{a^i},g\rangle,
\]

then clear one common denominator. Orthogonal projection onto the line spanned by g is nonzero exactly when its pairing with g is nonzero. Consequently

\[
e_{f,A}\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_i A(a^i)^{-1}v_i\ne0.
\]

The inverse character merely replaces j by -j and does not change the all-six nonvanishing criterion. This supplies the exact theorem-level bridge; the remaining task is to produce and certify g, the seven Gross points, and the weighted pairings.

## Required geometric and automorphic data

The following data must be supplied before the elementary certificate becomes an L-value certificate:

1. A presentation of the definite quaternion algebra ramified at 389 and infinity, a maximal/Eichler order of the required level, and the convention relating its right-ideal classes to the Brandt module.
2. The rational Jacquet–Langlands eigenfunction phi corresponding to the weight-two newform of E389.a1, with a certified eigenvalue identification. A raw Brandt-module vector must be translated to the **function** convention used by the toric period; stabilizer weights or dual-basis conventions cannot be silently ignored.
3. A specified optimal embedding of O_K into the relevant order, a base Gross point, and the seven CM translates under the verified class-group generator. Arbitrary seven vertices of the Brandt graph are not sufficient.
4. The seven resulting values, all in one common rational/integral normalization. Clearing a single common denominator is harmless; unrelated rescalings of individual entries are not.
5. The precise period formula, with this conductor-one character, this quaternion algebra and test vector, and nonzero local factors. The assumptions include the required inert/split factorization and coprimality of the discriminants and levels.

The arithmetic data N = 389, D_K = -71, and 389 inert in K make this a natural definite quaternionic setting, but they do not determine the CM map or the seven values.

For the eigenpacket-identification part there is a small explicit cutoff: the weight-two, level-389 Sturm bound is 65, since [SL_2(Z):Gamma_0(389)] = 390 and 2*390/12 = 65. Thus correctly constructed Hecke operators through 65 can certify the eigenvalues against the curve; prime operators through 61 suffice together with the verified Hecke recurrences. The [official Sage implementation](https://github.com/sagemath/sage/blob/develop/src/sage/modular/arithgroup/arithgroup_generic.py) includes `Gamma0(389).sturm_bound(2) = 65` as an example. A potentially cheaper first check is the T_2 = -2 eigenspace: if its dimension is exactly one in the correctly identified Brandt module, modularity and Jacquet–Langlands identify that unique line with the curve. That dimension has not been computed in this audit.

Changing the base Gross point cyclically shifts the vector and multiplies each period by a root of unity; replacing the class-group generator by another generator permutes the periods. Neither changes nonvanishing. Multiplying the entire eigenfunction by a nonzero rational number also preserves the criterion.

## Limits and fault cases

* A nonzero eigenvector on the whole Brandt module need not restrict nontrivially to these seven CM points. The vector v could be constant or zero. If so, this particular conductor-one auxiliary-character strategy does not certify nonvanishing; one may need another auxiliary conductor or field.
* Nonzero entries alone do not prove a nontrivial period is nonzero: (5,5,5,5,5,5,5) has all six nontrivial periods zero.
* Nonzero reduction of an unequal difference modulo 29 suffices to prove the corresponding integers differ, but zero reduction is inconclusive. The vector (29,0,0,0,0,0,0) has all six periods nonzero despite having zero reduction modulo 29.
* A period may be nonzero algebraically while its value at a selected 29-adic embedding is divisible by 29. The minimal-polynomial argument proves algebraic nonvanishing, not a uniform unit statement at 29.
* No actual CM values are embedded in the companion program. Every default vector is explicitly a fault or positive **fixture**, not data for E389.a1.

## Reproduction

```sh
python research/bsd-localization/logic/c7_period.py
python research/bsd-localization/logic/c7_period.py --values-json '[1,-1,0,0,0,0,0]'
```

The first command checks artificial fixtures, including a constant nonzero vector, a unit period, a period of norm seven, and a vector invisible modulo 29. The second evaluates the supplied integer vector. The output always states that geometric identification and the L-value bridge have not been checked by this program.

For each j it computes the exact degree-less-than-six remainder modulo Phi_7. It also multiplies the six conjugates to compute their rational norm as an integer, giving a second exact algebraic diagnostic. A nonzero norm is equivalent to the nonzero-period criterion, but a single unequal pair is the smaller certificate.
